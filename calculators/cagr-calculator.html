How it works

CAGR = (ending value ÷ beginning value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. It smooths uneven yearly returns into one steady annual rate. It suits a single investment with no extra deposits or withdrawals; for SIPs, use XIRR instead.

What this calculator does

CAGR turns the growth from a starting value to an ending value into one smooth yearly rate. It lets you compare investments held for different periods on equal terms.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 that becomes ₹2,50,000 over 5 years has an absolute return of 150% and a CAGR of about 20.11% a year.

Tips and limits

  • CAGR ignores the ups and downs along the way; two investments with the same CAGR can have very different risk.
  • It assumes no money was added or withdrawn. For SIPs or irregular cash flows use XIRR.

FAQs

What is a good CAGR?

Compare it with the alternatives: FDs near 7%, inflation near 5–6%, and long-term equity funds in the low teens in good periods. Past returns do not guarantee future ones.

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes, when the ending value is lower than the beginning value, the CAGR is negative. This calculator needs a positive ending value.
