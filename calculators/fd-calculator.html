How it works

Maturity = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t), where m is the number of compounding periods per year. Most banks compound FD interest quarterly. Senior citizens typically get about 0.50% extra, but the exact premium varies by bank, so check your bank’s rate card. TDS, if any, is not deducted here.

What this calculator does

A fixed deposit pays a fixed rate for a fixed period. This calculator shows the maturity amount and interest after compounding at your chosen frequency. Select the senior citizen option to add the usual 0.50% extra rate.

Worked example

A ₹1,00,000 deposit at 7% for 5 years with quarterly compounding matures at about ₹1,41,478, earning ₹41,478 in interest. The same deposit compounded yearly would mature at ₹1,40,255, a little less.

Tips and limits

  • Interest is taxable and your bank may deduct TDS, so your net return is lower than shown.
  • Rates differ between banks and tenures. Enter the rate on your own deposit receipt or rate card.
  • Premature withdrawal usually reduces the rate; that is not modelled here.

FAQs

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes, it is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Banks deduct TDS when interest crosses the threshold set by the tax rules.

Does quarterly compounding earn more than yearly?

Slightly, because interest is added to the principal more often.
