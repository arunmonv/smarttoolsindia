How it works

In an 11+1 style scheme you pay for n months and the jeweller adds bonus instalments, so you redeem (n + bonus) × instalment against jewellery. The effective return treats each instalment as paid at the start of a month and finds the monthly rate that grows them to the redemption value. Schemes usually apply only to jewellery purchases at the shop, may exclude making charges or have minimum purchase terms, so read the scheme rules and check the jeweller’s reputation.

What this calculator does

Many jewellers run monthly savings schemes in which you pay for 11 months and the jeweller adds the 12th instalment. This calculator shows the bonus, the total redemption value, how much gold it buys and the effective yearly return.

Worked example

Paying ₹5,000 a month for 11 months means ₹55,000 paid. With a one-instalment bonus the redemption value is ₹60,000, a gain of ₹5,000. At ₹7,500 per gram that buys about 8 g of gold, and the effective return works out to about 18.8% a year, because the instalments are paid over time while the bonus is a lump.

Tips and limits

  • The bonus is only valuable if you actually buy jewellery from that shop, and gold prices can change during the scheme. Compare with buying coins or digital gold.
  • Making charges, wastage and GST are usually charged on top. Ask for the scheme terms in writing, including what happens if you miss a month.
  • Prefer well-established jewellers and keep every receipt.

FAQs

Is a jewellery chit scheme safe?

It depends on the jeweller. Prefer established shops, get a written scheme card and receipts, and check what happens if you stop paying.

Are making charges and GST included?

Usually not. The redemption value is a credit against the bill, and making charges and GST on the jewellery are extra unless the scheme says otherwise.
