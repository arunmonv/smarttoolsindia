How it works

Gratuity = last drawn (basic + DA) × 15 × completed years ÷ 26 for employers covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act (÷ 30 otherwise). A part-year of six months or more counts as a full year. The result is capped at ₹20 lakh, the tax-free limit. Gratuity is normally payable after five years of continuous service; check your employment terms, since the new labour codes may allow earlier payment for fixed-term employees.

What this calculator does

Gratuity is a lump sum an employer pays when you leave after long service. This calculator applies the standard formula for employees covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act and the variant for those who are not.

Worked example

With a last drawn basic + DA of ₹50,000 and 8 years 4 months of service, 8 years are counted. Gratuity = 50,000 × 15 × 8 ÷ 26 = about ₹2,30,769. At 8 years 6 months, 9 years would be counted and the amount would rise to about ₹2,59,615.

Tips and limits

  • Gratuity is generally payable only after five years of continuous service; exceptions include death or disablement.
  • The amount is capped at ₹20 lakh. Check your offer letter or company policy for any better terms.
  • Rules for fixed-term employees are changing under the new labour codes; confirm with your HR team.

FAQs

Why divide by 26?

A month is taken as 26 working days, and gratuity is 15 days’ wages for each year of service.

Is gratuity taxable?

Gratuity for private employees covered by the Act is exempt up to ₹20 lakh; any amount above it is taxable.
