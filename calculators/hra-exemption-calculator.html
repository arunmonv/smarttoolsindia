How it works

The exempt HRA is the least of: (1) the HRA you actually receive, (2) rent paid minus 10% of basic salary plus DA, and (3) 50% of that salary in a metro or 40% elsewhere. The rest of the HRA is taxable. This exemption is available only in the old tax regime. The 50% limit applies to the eight metros from FY 2026-27 (earlier it applied to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai only), so choose the 40% option for an earlier year in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune or Ahmedabad.

What this calculator does

If you receive HRA and pay rent, part of the allowance can be tax-free under the old regime. The exemption is the least of three amounts: the HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of basic salary plus DA, and 50% or 40% of basic salary plus DA depending on the city. The calculator shows all three so you can see which one limits you.

Worked example

With basic + DA of ₹40,000 a month, HRA of ₹20,000 and rent of ₹18,000 in a metro: HRA received is ₹2,40,000 a year; rent minus 10% of salary is ₹1,68,000; 50% of salary is ₹2,40,000. The least is ₹1,68,000, so that amount is exempt and ₹72,000 of HRA is taxable.

Tips and limits

  • Keep rent receipts and the rent agreement. Give the landlord’s PAN if yearly rent is above ₹1 lakh.
  • No HRA exemption is available under the new tax regime.
  • Rules on which cities count as metros changed from FY 2026-27, so choose the option that matches the year you are calculating for.

FAQs

Do I need rent receipts?

Keep rent receipts and a rent agreement. If yearly rent exceeds ₹1 lakh, your employer will usually ask for your landlord’s PAN.

Can I claim HRA in the new regime?

No. HRA exemption, like most deductions, is available only under the old regime.
