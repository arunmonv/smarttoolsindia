How it works

New regime slabs: nil up to ₹4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% in ₹4 lakh steps above it, with a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free. Old regime: 5% from ₹2.5 lakh, 20% from ₹5 lakh, 30% above ₹10 lakh (higher exemption for seniors), with a ₹50,000 standard deduction, deductions such as 80C and 80D, and a rebate up to ₹5 lakh. A 4% cess is added. Surcharge for income above ₹50 lakh is not included.

What this calculator does

It calculates your income tax under both the old and the new regime for FY 2026-27 and tells you which one costs less. You enter your gross income, whether you are salaried, your age group, and the deductions you would claim in the old regime. The new regime allows only the standard deduction.

Worked example

Take a salaried person under 60 with ₹15,00,000 of income, ₹1,50,000 under 80C and ₹25,000 under 80D. Old regime: taxable income ₹12,75,000, tax with cess about ₹2,02,800. New regime: taxable income ₹14,25,000, tax with cess about ₹97,500. The new regime saves about ₹1,05,300 here.

At ₹12,75,000 of salary the new regime tax is zero, because the standard deduction brings taxable income to ₹12,00,000 and the rebate wipes out the tax.

Assumptions and limits

  • Resident individuals only. Surcharge on income above ₹50 lakh is not included.
  • Capital gains, special-rate income and the marginal relief at the old-regime rebate limit are not modelled.
  • Salaried standard deduction: ₹75,000 (new) and ₹50,000 (old).
  • Slabs can change in future budgets. Check the Income Tax Department website before filing your return.

FAQs

Which regime is better for me?

The new regime usually wins unless your old-regime deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest) are large. Enter your own figures above to compare.

Are these the latest slabs?

They match the slabs announced for FY 2026-27, which left the earlier slabs unchanged. Tax laws can change, so check the Income Tax Department website before filing.
