How it works

The loan is run month by month at the same EMI: interest is charged on the outstanding balance, the EMI (plus any extra amount) reduces it, and the one-time part-payment is deducted after the EMI number you choose. Because the EMI stays the same, the loan ends earlier. Compare the interest with and without prepayment to see the saving. Some lenders charge a prepayment fee on fixed-rate loans.

What this calculator does

It shows what happens when you pay part of your loan early. The EMI stays the same, the outstanding principal falls, and the loan closes sooner with less interest. You can enter a one-time part-payment, an extra amount every month, or both.

Worked example

For a ₹50,00,000 loan at 8.75% over 20 years, the EMI is about ₹44,186. A one-time part-payment of ₹5,00,000 after the 24th EMI cuts the tenure from 20 years to about 16 years 3 months, saving about 3 years 9 months and roughly ₹15.3 lakh of interest.

Tips and limits

  • Prepay as early as possible; interest is highest in the first years of a loan.
  • Floating-rate home loans for individuals usually carry no prepayment charge, but fixed-rate and business loans may. Ask your lender.
  • The calculator keeps the rate constant; a floating rate that changes will alter the figures.

FAQs

Should I reduce the EMI or the tenure?

Reducing the tenure usually saves more interest overall. Reducing the EMI eases monthly cash flow. This calculator shows the shorter-tenure option.

When is prepayment most effective?

Early in the loan, when most of each EMI is interest. The same amount prepaid in year 2 saves far more than in year 15.
