How it works

Future value = P × (1 + r)^t, where P is the amount invested, r the annual return (as a decimal) and t the years. Returns are compounded yearly and assumed steady; real market returns vary.

What this calculator does

This calculator estimates what a one-time investment can grow to when it earns a steady annual return, compounded yearly. Use it for a bonus, a maturity amount or any lump of money you plan to invest and leave untouched.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 invested for 10 years at an assumed 12% a year grows to about ₹3,10,585, an estimated gain of ₹2,10,585. Changing only the period to 20 years takes it past ₹9.6 lakh, which shows the effect of time.

Tips and limits

  • Returns on market-linked investments are not guaranteed and are rarely steady.
  • Taxes and fund charges are not deducted. Check the tax rules for the fund type you hold.

FAQs

Lumpsum or SIP – which is better?

A lumpsum puts all your money to work at once, while a SIP spreads it across market levels. SIP suits regular income; lumpsum suits a windfall.

Are taxes included?

No. Capital gains tax on mutual funds depends on the fund type and holding period and is not deducted here.
