How it works

Simple interest = P × r × t. Compound interest: A = P × (1 + r ÷ m)^(m × t), and the interest is A − P, where m is the compounding frequency per year. Simple interest is charged only on the original principal; compound interest is charged on principal plus interest already earned.

What this calculator does

It computes simple interest and compound interest on the same principal, rate and time, so the difference is visible straight away. Pick a compounding frequency for the compound result.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 at 8% for 5 years earns simple interest of ₹40,000 (total ₹1,40,000). With yearly compounding it earns about ₹46,933 (total ₹1,46,933). The extra ₹6,933 is interest earned on interest, and the gap widens as the years increase.

Tips

  • Check whether a loan or deposit quotes a simple or compounded rate before comparing offers.
  • For monthly loan repayments, use the EMI calculator instead, since EMIs reduce the balance every month.

FAQs

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest stays fixed on the principal. Compound interest grows each period because it is earned on earlier interest too.

Which do loans and FDs use?

Most bank FDs and loans use compounding or reducing balances. Some informal loans and a few schemes use simple interest.
