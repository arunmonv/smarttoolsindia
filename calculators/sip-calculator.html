How it works

Maturity value = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is the monthly instalment, i the monthly return (annual % ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of months. Instalments are assumed at the start of each month. Mutual fund returns are not guaranteed.

What this calculator does

A SIP (systematic investment plan) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. This calculator estimates the value of those instalments at the end of your chosen period, assuming a steady annual return. It shows how much you put in, how much the investment may earn and the total maturity value.

Worked example

A SIP of ₹5,000 a month for 10 years at an assumed 12% a year means 120 instalments and ₹6,00,000 invested. The estimated maturity value is about ₹11,61,695, so the estimated gain is about ₹5,61,695. Almost half of the final amount comes from compounding, which is why starting early matters.

Tips and limits

  • Use a conservative return for planning, such as 10%, and treat anything higher as a bonus.
  • Real fund returns rise and fall each year, so the actual value will differ from this steady-rate illustration.
  • Expense ratio, exit load and capital gains tax are not deducted here.

FAQs

What return should I assume for a SIP?

Equity funds have historically delivered roughly 10–12% a year over long periods, but returns vary and are not guaranteed. A conservative figure is safer for planning.

Is the result guaranteed?

No. SIPs in market-linked funds can gain or lose value. The calculator shows a steady-return illustration only.
